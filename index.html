The Story

From Khandra to the World Stage

Born on 24 December 1997 in Khandra village, Panipat, Haryana, Neeraj Chopra picked up a javelin almost by chance — sent to a local stadium as a kid, he found a sport instead of a routine, and never really put it down again.

He rose fast: a World U20 title and record at seventeen, Commonwealth and Asian Games gold by twenty, and then, on 7 August 2021 in Tokyo, a throw of 87.58 metres that made him the first Indian ever to win an Olympic gold medal in athletics. He now also holds the honorary rank of Subedar in the Indian Army.

His approach has barely changed at any stage of the climb: obsess over the process, stay coachable, and let the distance take care of itself.

The Journey

Milestones

  1. 2016

    World U20 Champion

    Wins gold at the World U20 Championships in Bydgoszcz, Poland, with a throw of 86.48 m — a new U20 world record.

  2. 2018

    Commonwealth & Asian Games Gold

    Gold at the Commonwealth Games in Gold Coast and the Asian Games in Jakarta, establishing himself as Asia's best.

  3. 2021

    Olympic Gold — Tokyo

    A throw of 87.58 m on 7 August makes him the first Indian to win an Olympic gold medal in athletics, and only the second individual gold for India ever.

  4. 2022

    Silver at Worlds, Diamond League Champion

    Silver at the World Championships in Eugene, then becomes the first Indian to win the Diamond League final, in Zurich.

  5. 2023

    World Champion — Budapest

    Gold at the World Athletics Championships, the first for an Indian javelin thrower, followed by gold at the Asian Games in Hangzhou.

  6. 2024

    Olympic Silver — Paris, New Personal Best

    Silver at the Paris Olympics, then a new personal best of 89.94 m days later at the Lausanne Diamond League.

One Throw. A Nation Watching.

When his javelin landed at 87.58 metres in Tokyo, it didn't just win a medal — it rewrote what Indian athletics believed was possible. Stadiums across the country now teach javelin throw to kids who, a few years ago, had never held one.
